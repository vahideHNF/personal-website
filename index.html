Intercity · Bidding

Giving drivers feedback before they bid

Finding. 84–88% of intercity drivers had tried bidding, but only 49–54% were satisfied with it. Drivers bid blind, with no idea whether a price would win, so they overbid or gave up. 77–89% of dissatisfied passengers said drivers bid two to three times the standard fare. One fixed bidding limit applied to every car, which drivers saw as unfair.

What changed. A live win-chance indicator based on price, ETA, rating and driver tier, plus driver tiers (Premium / Standard / Economy) with bidding limits that depend on car model and year.

Intercity · Driver periodic survey

Why drivers cancel after accepting

Finding. One of the main reasons intercity drivers cancelled after accepting was not knowing how many passengers were coming, how much luggage they had, or exactly when the trip started.

What changed. The request flow was redesigned so passengers set passenger count, luggage and time clearly, and drivers see them before accepting.

A second round of the survey led to a verified passenger badge before acceptance (+1.38% ride acceptance). It also showed drivers the passenger's phone number after acceptance on scheduled rides. That cut passenger cancellations before the start by 2.75% and raised finished rides by 17.8% among rides where the driver arrived on time.

Carpool · Passenger flow

Making the carpool wait less uncertain

Finding. I walked through the passenger carpool flow (request, matching, accepting, driver assigned). Passengers got no sign of progress, couldn't see who they were sharing with, and didn't trust the pickup information. That uncertainty made them cancel early.

What changed. Both passengers' pins and the stop order now appear when the ride is accepted, and the waiting page was redesigned to show clear status while a match is found.

Ride experience · Rating

Can we trust the star ratings?

Finding. 17–22% of passengers submitted a rating they didn't mean. The causes were a confusing screen, dismissing it in a rush, and rating late: 40–45% only rated just before their next ride. That made ratings a weak measure of driver performance.

What changed. We designed and tested rating during the ride, closer to the experience, and asked for a reason whenever someone gives one star.

Intercity · Offer card

A map in the offer card

Finding. Many intercity cancellations happened because drivers misjudged where the origin or destination city actually was before accepting.

What changed. I proposed a map view in the driver's offer card so the route is clear at a glance, and the team shipped it.

Research operations

Getting people to answer our surveys

Finding. Only 0.9% of users we texted answered our quantitative studies, which made results shaky and SMS costly. I traced the problem to the invitation message itself.

What changed. I rewrote the SMS invitation. Response rates went up five to six times, which gave every later survey more reliable data at a lower cost per answer.

Intercity · Conjoint

What passengers weigh in a bid

The research team's first conjoint study. It measured how much price, ETA, car type and other parts of the bid card matter to intercity passengers, and replaced less precise methods for bid card decisions.

Intercity · Bid card

A feature we didn't need to build

The research showed that a driver's number of completed rides doesn't change whether passengers accept a bid, so the feature was dropped. That saved engineering and product time.

Intercity · Pricing

Narrowing the bid range

Passenger survey findings on overpricing led to an experiment with lower price multipliers (max 2.5× → 2.2×). Fulfillment rose 1.72% and finish/accept rose 3.8%.

Intercity · Driver behaviour

Why drivers take intercity rides from Golchin

Full-time drivers prefer to see city and intercity offers side by side in Golchin. The study warned that removing intercity offers from Golchin would likely lower acceptance.

Scheduled rides · Findability
