Digikala · Seller advertising · 2022–2024

One decision per step: getting more Digikala sellers to run Lightning Deal campaigns

Lightning Deal was one of Digikala's biggest advertising products for sellers, but most sellers never used it, and more than half of those who tried gave up halfway. I found out why. The redesign raised the number of campaigns and nearly doubled the share of sellers who finished the flow.

My role
UX Researcher
Team
Product, design and UX writing
Company
Digikala, Iran's largest e-commerce marketplace
Methods
Analytics, interviews, survey, usability tests

In short

  • Problem
    Lightning Deal brought in a quarter of seller-panel revenue, yet only 38% of eligible sellers used it, and only 42% of those who started a campaign finished.
  • Insight
    Sellers didn't know the service existed, couldn't tell how it worked, or didn't know which products and time slots would pay off. The flow asked them to make too many decisions at once.
  • Impact
    A redesigned flow with one decision per step and personalised hints: +17% campaigns, funnel success up from 42% to 74%.

01 — The problemA big product most sellers weren't using

Lightning Deal is a short promotion: a seller offers a lower price on one product until it sells out or the time runs out, and the deal appears on Amazing Offers, Digikala's most-viewed page. To get a slot, sellers pay to join an auction.

The service brought in 25% of the seller panel's revenue, but only 38% of sellers used it, mostly the same experienced group. The business goals were clear: 20% more campaigns, 40% more revenue from the service, and a 30% better funnel success rate. What was stopping the other sellers?

02 — My roleWhat I owned

I ran the research: analytics review, interviews, the survey and two rounds of usability testing. I then worked with the designer, product manager and UX writer to turn the findings into solutions.

03 — ApproachFrom where sellers drop off, to why, to how many

  • Analytics (Google Analytics and Hotjar) to find where sellers dropped off and form first assumptions.
  • Interviews with 37 sellers to understand why, across three segments: sellers who had created campaigns, sellers who had tried and failed, and potential users who had never tried. These were sellers who had turned down Digikala's free Amazing promotion but used other paid ad services.
  • A survey of 390 sellers in the same segments (95% confidence, 5% margin of error) to measure how common each interview finding was.
  • Usability tests with 15 sellers to evaluate the new design, then fix it and test again.

04 — What we foundThe funnel told us where, sellers told us why

1. Sellers reached the auction, then stalled

Only 42.2% of sellers who started finished the funnel. Hotjar showed that 85% reached the auction step, but there only a quarter of clicks were on choosing a time slot, and just 4% clicked "Continue". The page asked sellers to decide too much at once.

2. Many sellers didn't know it existed, or how it worked

Among sellers who had never created a campaign, 27–36% didn't know about the service at all. Another 20–29% had heard of it and wanted to try, but found how it worked vague, and the description didn't help.

3. Sellers who tried didn't know what would pay off

41–50% of sellers who had used it weren't happy with the profit, and weren't sure which of their products suited a Lightning Deal or which time slot got the most traffic for that kind of product. They were guessing, and when a guess didn't pay off they stopped.

05 — From insight to decisionsMake each step one clear decision

In brainstorming sessions with design, product and UX writing, we turned the findings into two main changes:

  • A redesigned flow that reorganised the steps so sellers make just one decision on each step.
  • Dynamic hints and new content on the start page that give sellers personalised advice based on their own data and actions, such as which products and time slots are likely to work, so they can decide with data instead of guessing.

Then I ran usability tests with two groups: experienced sellers (four or more campaigns in the past three months) and potential users who had never used the service. One task asked: "Your colleague has never used Digikala's ad services. Help them join Lightning Deal with two of their products. Which of their products would suit it best?" We fixed the issues it revealed and tested again to raise the success rate.

07 — What I learnedReflection

Use each method for what it's good at.Analytics showed where sellers stalled, interviews explained why, and the survey showed how common each reason was. Skipping any one of them would have left us guessing.
Talk to the people who never showed up.The biggest gap, sellers who didn't know the service existed, only appeared because we deliberately recruited potential users who had never tried it.
Too many decisions is a design problem, not a user problem.Sellers weren't careless. The page asked them to make several uncertain decisions at once, and splitting them up did more than any amount of explanation.
