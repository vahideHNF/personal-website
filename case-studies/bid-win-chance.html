Snapp! · Marketplace & pricing · 2025–2026

Why were drivers bidding two to three times the fare?

On long-distance trips, drivers can bid their own price. Both sides were losing trust in it. This is how research turned guessing into a live win-chance signal, and brought prices down.

My role
UX Researcher (lead)
Team
Product managers, designers, data team
Timeline
Autumn 2025 – summer 2026
Methods
Driver & passenger surveys, A/B tests

Numbers on this page are illustrative. Study data is confidential under NDA, so these figures are changed. The findings, the direction of every change and the decisions are real. The real measured results of the shipped changes are listed in my resume.

In short

  • Problem
    Most drivers had tried bidding but only half liked it, and passengers saw bids at two to three times the fare.
  • Insight
    Drivers weren't greedy, they were bidding blind, under one bid limit for every car.
  • Impact
    A live win-chance indicator and driver tiers: +3.6% bids per request, −5.5% average bid price.
  1. 1 It started with a survey

    Drivers loved the idea of bidding, not the experience

    The first long-distance driver survey showed almost everyone had tried bidding. Only half were happy with it.

    82%of drivers had tried bidding
    51%were satisfied with it
  2. 2 Then passengers said the same

    Passengers saw prices they couldn't trust

    4 in 5 unhappy passengers said drivers bid two to three times the standard fare.

  3. 3 The diagnosis

    Drivers weren't greedy. They were bidding blind.

    GuessDriver picks a price with no signal
    →
    RejectedNo feedback on why
    →
    ReactGive up, or overbid next time

    ↺ and the loop repeats, on top of one bid limit for every car

  4. 4 What we built

    Show drivers what wins, and set fair limits by car

    Your bidLong-distance trip
    1,250,000
    Chance of winningHigh
    Send bid
    Premiumnewer, comfortable cars
    Standardmost cars
    Economyolder cars

    Each tier gets its own bid limits, set by car model and year.

  5. 5 Measured on real rides

    Prices came down, and drivers kept bidding

    • Average bid price−5.5%
    • Bids per request+3.6%
    • Passenger cancellations−0.7%

    Change vs. the old bidding flow, A/B test · plus 3 seconds faster for passengers to accept a bid

  6. 6 A trade-off we tested

    A narrower price range: fewer bids, more finished rides

    GainCost
    • Finish / accept+3.2%
    • Fulfillment+1.5%
    • Requests receiving a bid−2.0%
    • Acceptance−1.3%

    Top price multiplier lowered from 2.5× to 2.2×, A/B test

  7. 7 Checking back with drivers

    Fewer drivers gave up after a rejected bid

    The next complaint to solve: competition between drivers (see the driver survey).
