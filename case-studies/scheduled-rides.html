Why do drivers cancel a ride minutes after accepting it?

Scheduled rides let passengers book a long trip days ahead. Too many drivers dropped them within two minutes. The answer wasn't who the drivers were, but what had happened to them before.

My role
UX Researcher (lead)
Team
Product managers, designers, data analyst
Timeline
3 weeks, summer 2026
Methods
Two-segment survey, behavioural data

Numbers on this page are illustrative. Study data is confidential under NDA, so these figures are changed. The findings, the direction of every change and the decisions are real. The real measured results of the shipped changes are listed in my resume.

In short

  • Problem
    About half of after-accept cancellations on scheduled rides happened within minutes, with six possible causes.
  • Insight
    Quick cancellers weren't a different kind of driver. They had been let down by passengers before.
  • Impact
    Passenger contact details after accepting: +15% finished rides, −3.1% passenger cancellations.

  2. 2 Too many suspects

    Earlier interviews gave us six possible causes

    No passenger details after accepting
    A 15-minute contact rule nobody had explained
    A fare range that made income hard to plan
    Passengers cancelling at the last minute
    No way to book a ride back home
    A better on-demand ride showing up

    All plausible. The team couldn't build all six, so which ones actually drive cancellations?
